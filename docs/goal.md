The Problem

Africa is rich with creativity, yet many talented artists, musicians, poets, and storytellers never receive recognition beyond their local communities. We know artists like JH Pierneef, because his work was documented and preserved, but there are likely countless others across Africa whose stories and talents may never be captured.

As AI becomes increasingly influential in how content is created and discovered, there is a risk that voices not already represented online will remain invisible.

The Approach

With KeMonate (Setswana for cool, nice), anyone could use a mobile phone to record an interview, story, song, upload of painting in their own language. AI would then help transcribe, translate, organize, and transform that content into biographies, articles, podcasts, documentaries, or digital archives, while ensuring the creator remains at the center of the story.

The goal is not to use AI to replace creativity, but to help preserve, celebrate, and share authentic human stories.

What Excites Me

What excites me most is the possibility of uncovering extraordinary people whose stories would otherwise never be told. Somewhere in a small town or village there may be a painter, musician, writer, or storyteller whose talent rivals the great names we know, but whose work has never been seen beyond their community.

I want to use AI to help find those hidden voices and ensure that their stories, knowledge, and creativity are preserved for future generations.

The Pitch:
KeMonate uses AI to help communities capture, preserve, and share Africa's untold stories, ensuring that local voices are celebrated before they are lost
